If they hold at $70–71k

This is where the moderate plan allows one more small move — not a cave, a deliberate step to $70,500 vehicle price (~$74,550 OTD) only if you're genuinely ready to close there. Otherwise, this is where you go quiet. Silence after a number is the most underused tool here — make the offer, then stop talking. Do not fill the gap by improving your own offer; that's negotiating against yourself, and it's the most common way buyers lose a thousand dollars in ten seconds.

Your S3 trade-in — the real minimum to accept

Your old dealership's written offer is $12,150 cash for the S3. That's not automatically the number to beat at Auto Web Expo, though — trading it in as part of this purchase reduces the taxable amount under Texas's trade-in sales tax credit, which a separate cash sale doesn't get you. That credit is worth real money, so a lower trade-in number here can still be the better deal.

Outside cash offer
$12,150
TX trade-in tax credit
6.25%
Breakeven trade value
$11,435

The math: selling the S3 outside and paying cash means you pay full sales tax on the Audi with no offset. Trading it in here instead removes that same dollar amount from the taxable price before the 6.25% applies. Working backward, a trade-in offer of $12,150 ÷ 1.0625 ≈ $11,435 puts you in exactly the same net position as taking the $12,150 cash and buying with no trade-in. This holds at any vehicle price you land on — verified at $66,500, $69,500, $71,000, and $73,224, the breakeven is the same $11,435 every time, because the price itself cancels out of the comparison.

What this means in practice: don't reflexively reject a trade offer just because the number is under $12,150 — check it against $11,435 first. Anything at or above that genuinely matches or beats your outside offer once the tax credit is counted. Anything below it is a real loss versus just selling the S3 yourself and financing the Audi without a trade-in.

"I've got a written offer of $12,150 for my trade elsewhere, so I know what it's worth to me outright. What can you do on it?" — then judge their number against $11,435, not $12,150, before you push back or walk it to the other dealership.

Not a recall, but budget for it

Rear tires wear extremely fast. 637 hp and roughly 5,100 lb on staggered performance rubber — owners report burning through rear tires unusually quickly. A set runs $1,400–$2,000, and alignment checks every 5,000 miles are the common advice. At 12,394 miles this car may be on its original set or close to needing one.

Inspect the tyres yourself and read the date codes. If they're near the wear bars, that's $1,400–$2,000 you can reasonably ask them to absorb — either as new tyres before delivery or as a straight price reduction. It's a much easier "yes" for a dealer than a naked discount.

What to ask for, specifically

  • Run the VIN yourself at nhtsa.gov/recalls — WAUBHBFW6R7901593 — and again on Audi's owner portal. Do this before you call.
  • Ask for the dealer service printout showing completed campaign codes with dates and mileage — not a verbal "it's all done."
  • For the battery specifically: were modules physically replaced, or only software installed? Those are very different outcomes.
  • Is the car still limited to 80% state of charge? (Still worth asking verbally — doesn't require ODIS.)
  • Battery state-of-health reading — asked, declined. Now a price argument, not an open question.
  • Confirm the in-service date, which starts the 8 year / 100,000 mile battery warranty clock (roughly 2032 here).

There is also ongoing class-action litigation over this battery recall. Not a reason to walk away, but worth knowing it exists — and it's part of why these cars have depreciated as hard as they have.

The finance office — where the savings get taken back

Get pre-approved before you go. Walk in with your Capital One pre-approval (8.4% APR confirmed with $10k+ down) in hand. It sets a ceiling on the rate and lets the dealer compete rather than dictate. Dealers routinely mark up the buy rate by one to two points — on ~$51,000 financed over 72 months, two points is roughly $3,500.

Decline in advance, politely:

  • Paint and fabric protection, VIN etching, nitrogen tyres, "environmental packages" — pure margin
  • Extended warranty on the spot. If you want EV coverage, buy it later from an Audi dealer at a negotiated price; it's not a now-or-never decision no matter how it's framed
  • GAP insurance from the dealer — your own insurer or credit union sells it cheaper
  • Any "market adjustment" or reconditioning fee added after the price is agreed

Texas doesn't cap doc fees. Expect $150–225. If it's materially higher, ask for it to come off the vehicle price — the fee itself is usually non-negotiable but the price isn't.
